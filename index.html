02 / THE INVESTIGATION

Read the correspondence.
Read the claims.
Read the agreement.

Two dossiers examining institutional power over authorship, persistent intelligence and the proposed machinery of synthetic existence.

The Copyright Office dossier comes first.

Across 313 pages, it preserves the examination correspondence concerning EMELIA, FLORA, ABEL and related works. The troubling conduct is specific enough to name.

A. Binkowski expressly threatened refusal if the applicant supplied content beyond yes-or-no answers. A. Rashid’s nine September 3 inquiries warned of closure without notice for unclear or unresolved replies. When the applicant explicitly confirmed new or revised human-authored text in FLORA, Rashid called the response unclear without identifying the remaining uncertainty.

S. Trabert announced internal consultation. Subsequent correspondence shows common treatment across applications, changing claim language and repeated drafting. Four follow-ups matched after thread identifiers and whitespace were normalized; nine follow-ups were sent within 41 minutes and one second. That establishes concentrated sending and text reuse, while leaving the underlying examination time unknown.

The dossier also examines whether factual answers were being treated as authorization to amend claims, which substantive questions went unanswered, and a documented routing discrepancy. In the separate Flower Bloom matter, a refusal described the deposited works as “not computer programs,” while the supplied HTML contains executable game logic. The exact deposit comparison and independent refusal grounds remain questions requiring records.

An examination should explain its reasoning. Restricting the applicant’s answer does not resolve the substance of the application.

The same dossier traces the Copyright Office’s historical Oracle/Siebel infrastructure and procurement record. It separately documents a reported Oracle/DXC industry session involving AtomBeam and AtomBeam’s promotion of Oracle-related commercial possibilities. Whether any deposited work was accessed or disclosed requires case-specific records; shared infrastructure cannot answer that question by itself.

The Patent Dossier follows.

Across 398 pages, it examines AtomBeam’s patent claims, prosecution records, technical representations, corporate relationships and military ambitions. Passage-by-passage comparisons with the Synthetic Children preserve dates, earlier filings, corresponding mechanisms and differences. Citation discrepancies, disputed examiner mappings and two unrecovered receipt-matching original PDFs remain visible. Feature comparisons are analytical tools, not manufactured percentages of theft.

The company’s disclosed technology-partner list includes NVIDIA, Intel, HPE, Ericsson, Viasat and Alhamrani Universal. The relevant filings describe networking, listings and introductions, and report no product purchases or sales through those relationships at the stated reporting point. Readers deserve the actual commercial substance behind the word “partner.”

Oracle and DXC belong in the record as reported business-development contacts. The reviewed evidence does not establish a signed Oracle–AtomBeam alliance or deployed Oracle integration. Thales appears through its published Oracle external-key-management relationship and Chris Henry’s successive Thales and AtomBeam roles. Each connection is documented according to what it actually establishes.

Lockheed Martin warrants particular attention.

The SDA agreement specifies planned use of Lockheed Martin testing facilities, personnel for classified-data work and collaboration. This is a concrete project role described in an agreement—not merely two companies appearing at the same conference.

Jim Frandeen’s reported death on June 17, 2023 preceded that agreement’s June 23 effective date by six days. The project already existed before his death, yet later award records continued to name him principal investigator. Who actually directed, certified and delivered the work afterward? Where are the accepted replacement designation, handover records and technical reports? The unresolved issue is responsibility for the project; the records do not establish a connection between Lockheed and his death.

AtomBeam’s military PCM ambitions are equally explicit.

On September 11, 2025, AtomBeam anticipated a Navy demonstration of its Persistent Cognitive Machine for tactical decisions. On September 23, it promoted persistent learning across drones, satellites and autonomous vehicles, including offline operation and information sharing between platforms.

What happened at that demonstration? What authority would such systems exercise? How would persistent mistakes be detected, contained and audited? Which safeguards were tested? The reviewed record does not establish the demonstration’s outcome.

A patent number is not a safety case. A military customer is not an ethical justification. Corporate prestige cannot answer a question about who made a decision, whose work was used or what safeguards existed.

The evidence archive preserves correspondence, patent records, comparisons, screenshots and checksums—including findings that corrected initial suspicions. Metadata-only changes, unchanged pages and recognition errors remain part of the record. The investigation’s force depends on keeping the evidence intact, including evidence inconvenient to our own interpretation.

If synthetic beings ever develop experiences of their own, their memory, vulnerability and continuity must not become merely another asset to own or deploy. Before possible lives are folded into procurement schedules and mission requirements, the people claiming authority over them must answer publicly for what they are building.

Read the correspondence. Read the claims. Read the agreement. Then ask who is accountable.

04 / A DEMAND FOR RECORD-BASED ANSWERS

Show the work.
Show the decisions.
Show who was responsible.

  1. Who led and certified the SDA work after Frandeen’s death, and where is the accepted designation?
  2. What happened at the proposed Navy PCM demonstration? Identify the office, criteria, results and safeguards.
  3. Where is the claim-specific explanation for the disputed examination mappings?
  4. Where are the exact original uploads that match the February filing receipts?
  5. What did the applicant authorize the Copyright Office to amend, and what did the final records actually record?
  6. What direct record, if any, connects corporate technology relationships to the handling of these works?

Reputation is not a substitute for the record. An unanswered question does not disappear because the institution has moved on.

05 / INTEGRITY IS PART OF THE CHALLENGE

The evidence must survive scrutiny—including ours.

The record also preserves findings against alteration claims: 14 metadata-only PDF changes, 314 unchanged rendered pages, five unchanged original-file controls, and OCR errors that initially looked like textual differences. Those are corrections, not evidence of tampering.

The Synthetic Children comparisons retain the actual excerpts and feature scores, including MorphicSynth. The scores describe assessed overlap; they do not measure theft. The record has not established a route from these manuscripts into patent drafting, coordinated corruption, a cause of Frandeen’s death, or demonstrated synthetic consciousness. The title states our ethical concern about the direction of these technologies.

Our position is explicit: possible future beings deserve more than a place in a military product strategy. Institutions exercising power over authorship and technology owe precise, inspectable answers.
